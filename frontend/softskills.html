MASS & DAY-1 DRIVE ELIMINATOR

Campus Group Discussion (GD) Playbook & Strategy

Companies like Deloitte, TCS, Mu Sigma, Wipro, and banks eliminate up to 60% of applicants in the GD round. Learn how to speak with high impact without shouting or dominating.

1. The Structured Initiator

Highest Risk • Highest Reward

Opening the discussion sets the frame. Never start with an aggressive opinion. Instead, define the topic scope, introduce 2–3 structured angles (e.g. Economic, Ethical, Technological), and invite the group.

Winning Opener Phrase: "Good morning everyone. The topic before us is 'AI Automation in Banking'. To structure our discussion, I propose we look at it through three lenses: operational efficiency, customer trust, and job transition."

2. The Data & Metric Driver

Safest High-Scoring Role

When others argue emotion, you anchor the discussion with facts, case studies, or quantitative benchmarks. Recruiters love candidates who bring signal over noise.

Winning Data Phrase: "Building on what Aniket mentioned regarding costs, according to the recent RBI report, digital payment transactions grew by 44% year-over-year, which validates that customer readiness is already established."

3. The Harmonizer & Moderator

Leadership Score Booster

When two candidates enter a shouting match, calmly interject to summarize both points and pass the floor to quieter members. Shows emotional intelligence and leadership.

Winning Moderator Phrase: "Both points are valid: Rahul is emphasizing data privacy while Priya is highlighting speed. I also notice Sneha has been trying to share her thoughts; let's hear her perspective on this."

4. The Objective Summarizer

Closes the Deal

In the final 60 seconds, deliver a balanced summary representing the entire group's consensus, acknowledging both pros and cons without introducing your own personal new ideas.

Winning Closer Phrase: "To summarize the collective viewpoints of our group today, we agreed on two core benefits, identified one critical implementation hurdle, and concluded that gradual phasing is the optimal solution."
